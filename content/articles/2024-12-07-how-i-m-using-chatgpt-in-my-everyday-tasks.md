[ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com/?ref=hulry.com) has been one of the biggest innovations in tech in recent years.

The kind of work it can do to make our lives easier is mindboggling.

Initially, I hesitated to jump into this new tech, but once I connected the dots, I could see its true potential in my work and life.

Maybe you're in the same boat.

You might or might not have tried ChatGPT or other AI tools, thinking this will pass like just another trend.

But this one's different.

In this blog post, I'll demonstrate the various scenarios I've been using ChatGPT for my work and how you can make this tool more relatable to your life too.

Let's start by:

## Setting the right intention

I'll admit it.

When ChatGPT came out, I perceived it more as an amusement than a real asset.

Reading all the hyped opinions on Twitter about how AI will replace writers, designers and more with its creative solutions, I was sceptical about how it could help me in my day-to-day work.

I was sure I wouldn't replace my writing with AI-generated content.

Regardless of how good the generated texts are, nothing beats [sharing a personal story](https://hulry.com/firesides/) and the overall feeling of drafting, editing and publishing a good blog post.

But then came the "aha" moment.

I started thinking of ChatGPT as the assistant I've always wanted but never had, and it all started to make sense.

I was quickly coming up with potential use cases where I could plug ChatGPT into my regular workflows and make work easier for me.

For example, instead of asking ChatGPT to write articles for me, I could see it as a research partner helping me digest complex topics quickly.

So:

Before diving deep into this tool, you *must* understand the problems you want to solve with it.

Once you've identified the areas in your work and life where ChatGPT can help, developing ways to plug this tool into your existing workflows becomes straightforward.

With that in mind, let's look into some real examples of how I've been leveraging ChatGPT in my life lately.

## Dissecting complex topics and ideas

The most exciting way ChatGPT is of value for me is by being an excellent partner to study with.

Usually, studying a topic or a new concept would mean doing the following:

1. Search for the topic or idea
2. Collect a handful of blog posts, research papers, etc., on the topic
3. Go through each collected material to understand the core principles

And this is the *initial* groundwork.

As I read through ideas and solutions, I had follow-up questions and had to repeat the same process for each.

Now, with ChatGPT, this process has boiled down to this:

1. Ask ChatGPT to explain a topic or a concept in an easy-to-understand format and with some real-world analogies
2. Ask any follow-up questions on the same chat to dissect the topic further

It's like having a private tutor sitting with me, ready to answer my queries anytime.

## Reading through PDF documents in minutes

Research papers, brochures, terms and conditions of a shiny new credit card, all of this information are often distributed as lengthy PDFs that take manual labour and time to go through.

Thankfully, ChatGPT can turn any PDF into a chat that answers from the document's content in seconds.

Here's how:

When starting a new chat, you can attach a PDF document by clicking the attach button over here:

You can then type your question as usual, and ChatGPT will read the attached PDF document and answer from its contents.

For example, I recently got a credit card from the State Bank of India and wanted to understand the exclusions for getting cashback on my transactions.

Reading through the terms and conditions of this card and finding this information would've been time-consuming and dull.

## ChatGPT Plus is worth your money

Depending on where you live, $20/month could be a substantial amount of money to spend on an app.

Most of the advanced features, like access to their flagship model, **GPT-4o**, [setting custom instructions](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8096356-custom-instructions-for-chatgpt?ref=hulry.com) and the ability to attach files to your chat, are available even without a ChatGPT Plus subscription.

The catch is that these premium features are limited to a few uses daily.

Once you've hit your free quota, you lose the ability to attach files to your chat, and further answers will be downgraded to the lower-end GPT-3.5 model until your quota resets:

This is sufficient if you occasionally use ChatGPT or don't need to attach files to your chat.

The older GPT-3.5 model can answer most questions without a hiccup.

After I exhausted my free GPT-4o quota in the morning, I waited until the next refresh to pick up where I left off.

I didn't need the answers immediately, so I could get by without spending $20/month for the same capabilities.

However, if you heavily rely on ChatGPT and use it frequently throughout the day, then spending that extra money on getting a Plus subscription could earn you a decent return on your investment.

## The not-so-good parts of ChatGPT

Even though ChatGPT has access to the internet, it often hallucinates and confidently spits out inaccurate answers that make it challenging for research.

When I asked it to summarise the top announcements in the 2024 Microsoft Build conference, it came up with random items that weren't even the major announcements this year:

Also, if you notice carefully, it said that Microsoft is including support for the **new GPT-4.5 model** in their Azure service:

As of this writing, there have been no official announcements about this model. The latest model is [GPT-4o](https://openai.com/index/hello-gpt-4o/?ref=hulry.com), which ChatGPT doesn't seem to know about.

In another chat, I asked ChatGPT about a recent car crash incident that has been making headlines since last week.

ChatGPT swiftly came back with completely different incident details, which I don't even know if it's an actual event.

Even with real-time access to the internet, ChatGPT fumbles to make sense of current events and dangerously replies with made-up answers without hesitation.

Therefore, while ChatGPT is excellent for doing grunt work, I [use another AI tool called Perplexity for my research](https://hulry.com/what-is-perplexity-ai/).

Perplexity searches the web on every question and answers questions by collecting and summarising relevant search results, which makes it more accurate and reliable than ChatGPT.
